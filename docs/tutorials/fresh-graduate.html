Fresh graduate

Age 22–24. First job, ₹8,000/month to invest, and 35+ years until retirement. The most important thing at this stage isn't the amount — it's starting.

Who this is for

You've just started your first job. After rent, food, and EMIs, you can set aside a modest amount each month. You may have just enrolled in EPF through your employer and opened a PPF account on a friend's recommendation. You're not sure what return rate to expect from anything.

This walkthrough builds a realistic starter plan and shows you why starting early — even with a small amount — has an outsized effect on your final corpus.

Step-by-step

  1. Open the calculator and clear the default table

    Open the app. Delete all the default rows using the × button on the right of each row, so you start with an empty table.

  2. Add EPF / PF

    Click "+ Add an asset". Name it "EPF / PF". Current value: ₹0. Return %/yr: 8.2 (EPFO's current declared rate). % of monthly investment: 20 (your employer contributes 12% of basic automatically; treat your voluntary top-up as part of this weight).

  3. Add PPF

    Click "+ Add an asset". Name it "PPF". Current value: ₹0. Return %/yr: 7.25. % of monthly investment: 15. PPF has a 15-year lock-in, so it's a long-term holding — set and forget.

  4. Add SIP / mutual funds

    Click "+ Add an asset". Name it "SIP / mutual funds". Current value: ₹0. Return %/yr: 12 (a conservative estimate for a diversified large-cap or index fund over a 20+ year horizon). % of monthly investment: 65. This is your primary wealth-building vehicle at this stage.

  5. Set the contribution plan

    Monthly investment: ₹8,000. Flat for the first: 3 years (give yourself time to build an emergency fund before stepping up). Step up every: 2 years. Step-up amount: 10% (assume salary grows and you increase investments with it).

  6. Set the milestone years

    Change the milestone years to 10, 20, and 35 — these correspond to roughly age 33, 43, and 57.

  7. Read the projections

    Look at the three milestone panels. Notice how the 35-year number dwarfs the 10-year number — not because of large contributions, but because of compounding time. The SIP column in your asset table shows where most of the growth comes from.

  8. Toggle inflation adjustment

    Check "Show in today's rupees". The 35-year number shrinks significantly. This is the real purchasing power of your corpus at retirement — a more honest view of what the money will actually buy.

What the numbers mean

With ₹8,000/month, 10% step-ups every 2 years after 3 flat years, and these return rates, your projected corpus at 35 years is in the range of ₹3–5 Cr nominal (₹60L–₹1 Cr in today's money at 6% inflation). The exact number depends on the return rates holding up over time.

The key insight from this scenario: starting at 22 vs. 27 makes a larger difference than doubling your contribution amount. The 5 extra years of compounding on the SIP — even at ₹8,000/month — add more to the final corpus than increasing the monthly amount from ₹8,000 to ₹16,000 starting at 27.

Next step: Export your plan using the Export button. Save it as your year-1 baseline. In a year, update the current values with actual balances and compare your real growth to the projection.
