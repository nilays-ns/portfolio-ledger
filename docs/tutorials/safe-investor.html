Safe investor

FDs, PPF, and government bonds only. Predictable, capital-protected returns. This walkthrough shows what a fully conservative portfolio looks like — and what inflation quietly does to it over time.

Who this is for

You prioritise capital safety over maximum returns. You are uncomfortable with market volatility and prefer instruments where the return is known in advance — bank FDs, PPF, government bonds, NSC, and similar. You may be a retiree, a conservative saver, or someone who simply hasn't been able to stomach market ups and downs.

This walkthrough builds a conservative portfolio and delivers the key insight of safe investing: after inflation, the real return on "safe" instruments is often very low — sometimes near zero or negative.

  2. Set the contribution plan

    Monthly investment: ₹20,000. Flat for the first: 5 years. Step up every: 3 years. Step-up amount: 8%.

  3. Set milestones to 10, 20, 30

    Note the nominal 30-year total. Write it down.

  4. Enable the inflation toggle at 6%

    Now observe the real-terms 30-year total. Compare it to what you wrote down. At 7% nominal return and 6% inflation, your real return is approximately 1% per year — meaning your money barely keeps pace with inflation.

  5. Try inflation at 7%

    Drag the inflation slider to 7%. At this rate, your FD returns are completely wiped out by inflation. Your PPF at 7.25% earns a real return of only 0.25%/year — roughly nothing over 30 years in real terms.

    Add one more row: "SIP / mutual funds". Set current value to ₹0, return to 11%, contrib weight to 30. Redistribute — reduce FD weight from 30 to 10, reduce NSC from 10 to 5, reduce G-Sec from 15 to 5. Observe what adding even a small equity allocation does to the real-terms 30-year total.

What the numbers mean

A fully conservative portfolio at 7–8% nominal returns grows significantly in nominal terms over 30 years. The problem is inflation. At India's historical average of 5–7%, "safe" instruments provide a real return of 0–2% per year — which means your purchasing power barely grows.

This is not an argument against safe instruments — capital protection and predictability are genuine value. PPF and EPF are especially strong because their returns are tax-free, which improves the real post-tax return considerably compared to FDs (where interest is taxed as income).

The lesson from this walkthrough is that even a small allocation to higher-return assets (10–20% in equity mutual funds) can meaningfully improve long-run real returns without dramatically changing the risk profile of your overall portfolio.

Tax matters for "safe" instruments: FD interest is taxable as income. PPF and EPF maturity proceeds are tax-free. For a high-income earner in the 30% tax bracket, the effective post-tax return on an FD at 7% is only ~4.9% — well below inflation. This is a key reason financial planners recommend PPF over FDs for the fixed-income portion of a portfolio.
