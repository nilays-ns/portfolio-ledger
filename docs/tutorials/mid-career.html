Mid-career investor

Age 32–35. Decent corpus built up over 10 years, rising income, and 20+ years still ahead. Now is when portfolio diversification and step-up contributions matter most.

Who this is for

You've been investing for about a decade. You have a meaningful EPF balance from multiple jobs, a PPF account nearing its first maturity, a SIP running, some gold (bought for a wedding or as a hedge), and maybe a handful of individual stocks. Your income has grown and so has your investable surplus.

This walkthrough shows how to model a multi-asset portfolio and use the step-up feature to reflect the income growth you expect over the next decade.

Step-by-step

  1. Click "Load an example" then clear it

    This resets the table to a clean state with the right structure. Then delete all rows and re-enter them with the values above — entering your own numbers builds intuition for what each field does.

  2. Enter all five asset rows

    Add each asset with the values from the table above. Notice that the contribution weights don't add to 100 — that's fine. The weights are 15 + 10 + 50 + 15 + 10 = 100 in this case, but they'd work just as well if you used 3 + 2 + 10 + 3 + 2.

  3. Set the contribution plan

    Monthly investment: ₹40,000. Flat for the first: 2 years. Step up every: 2 years. Step-up amount: 15% (your income is growing faster than inflation at this stage of your career).

  4. Set milestones to 5, 15, 25

    These correspond roughly to age 38, 48, and 58 — a mid-term check-in, near-retirement, and retirement horizon.

  5. Observe the asset-level projections in the table

    Look at the "In 25y" column for each row. SIP should dominate because it gets the highest contribution weight and a high return rate. Stocks may project higher per-unit growth despite lower weight, because of the 14% return rate — notice how return rate and contribution weight interact.

  6. Try reducing the SIP return from 12% to 10%

    Change SIP's Return %/yr to 10 and observe how much the 25-year total changes. This is sensitivity analysis — a 2% difference in return rate over 25 years has a dramatic effect on the final number. This is why picking a realistic (not optimistic) return assumption matters.

  7. Enable the inflation toggle

    Check "Show in today's rupees". The real-terms 25-year number is the more honest figure to plan around. If your target is to have ₹5 Cr of today's purchasing power, this view tells you whether you're on track.

What the numbers mean

With ₹40,000/month, 15% step-ups, and these return assumptions, the 25-year nominal corpus should be in the range of ₹8–12 Cr. In today's money (6% inflation), that's roughly ₹2–3 Cr.

The step-up contribution has a large effect here. Try setting the step-up to 0 (no increase ever) and observe the difference — the step-up alone often adds 40–60% to the final corpus over a 25-year period.

Tip: This is a good time to run two scenarios — "aggressive" (14% SIP return, 15% step-up) and "conservative" (10% SIP return, 8% step-up). Export both and keep them. Your actual outcome will likely land somewhere between them.
